MUNDER DIFFLIN MULTI-AGENT SYSTEM - DESIGN NOTES
================================================

Files
-----
- project_starter.py         Agents, tools, and the test harness (run: python project_starter.py)
- diagrams/workflow_diagram.md   Architecture and request-lifecycle diagrams with the tool table
- test_results.csv           Results of the 20 requests in quote_requests_sample.csv


1. ARCHITECTURE
---------------
The system uses smolagents and four agents (the limit is five):

- Orchestrator Agent (gpt-4o): a ToolCallingAgent whose only "tools" are the three worker
  agents (managed_agents). It delegates in a fixed order, Inventory -> Quoting -> Sales, one
  agent per step (parallel tool calls are disabled), and writes the reply to the customer.
- Inventory Agent (gpt-4o-mini): matches requested items to the catalog, checks stock, and
  restocks from the supplier, through the check_inventory tool.
- Quoting Agent (gpt-4o-mini): prices the available items with calculate_quote (catalog unit
  prices plus a bulk discount) and explains the price using similar past quotes
  (search_quote_history).
- Sales Agent (gpt-4o-mini): re-checks stock, records the accepted order with fulfill_order,
  and reports the delivery date the inventory check confirmed.

All seven starter helpers are used by at least one tool: get_all_inventory, get_stock_level,
get_supplier_delivery_date, get_cash_balance, create_transaction, generate_financial_report,
and search_quote_history. The tool-to-helper mapping is in diagrams/workflow_diagram.md.

The Orchestrator uses a stronger model than the workers because it makes the workflow
decisions. With gpt-4o-mini it sometimes stopped after the inventory check or skipped the
sale; the workers' jobs are narrow enough for gpt-4o-mini.


2. HOW A REQUEST IS HANDLED
---------------------------
OrchestrationAgent.handle_request(request, request_date) runs one request:

1. handle_request stores the request date and text in REQUEST_CONTEXT, a per-request store the tools
   share. It is cleared when the request finishes.
2. The Orchestrator sends the items, as the customer wrote them, with quantities, their units,
   and the needed-by date to the Inventory Agent. The Inventory Agent calls check_inventory once.
   For each item the tool:
     a. matches it to the catalog and converts the quantity to catalog units, e.g. 500 reams
        -> 250,000 sheets (find_catalog_items),
     b. checks stock as of the request date (get_stock_level),
     c. restocks when stock is short of the request, or would fall below min_stock_level
        after the sale (restock_item). It orders the requested quantity plus the minimum
        level, but only if get_supplier_delivery_date is on or before the needed-by date and
        get_cash_balance covers the cost,
     d. returns a status: available, not carried, needs clarification (pack size unknown), or
        insufficient stock, with a note. An available item also gets a ready date: the request
        date when it is in stock, or the supplier delivery date when it had to be restocked.
   The rows and the needed-by date are saved in REQUEST_CONTEXT.
3. If no item is available, the Orchestrator declines politely and gives the reason for each
   item.
4. Otherwise the Quoting Agent prices the available items. calculate_quote takes the items and
   quantities from REQUEST_CONTEXT, applies catalog prices and the discount tier, and the agent
   adds a short rationale.
5. The Sales Agent records the order. fulfill_order prices it exactly like calculate_quote,
   checks stock, and writes one 'sales' transaction per line item, dated the request date. Its
   delivery date is the latest ready date from the inventory check; if that is after the
   needed-by date, nothing is recorded. The Sales Agent has no date tool of its own.
6. The Orchestrator replies with the quote, discount, delivery date, and the items that could
   not be supplied and why. Two final answer checks refuse the reply while available items are
   still unsold (check_available_items_sold), or while it leaves out the sale's delivery date,
   total, quantities, or unit prices, calls another date the customer's deadline, or contains a
   placeholder such as [Your Name] (check_reply_matches_order).


3. BUSINESS RULES
-----------------
- Catalog matching is strict on size and finish. An item matches only if the catalog item has
  the size the customer named (A4, A3, letter, 8.5"x11", 24"x36", ...) and its name covers every
  finish or material asked for (glossy, matte, colored, recycled, ...). Sizes are read with any
  inch mark (8.5"x11", 8.5'x11', 8.5 x 11), because agents rewrite the quotes. Examples:
    "heavy cardstock (white)"  -> Cardstock
    "A4 white printer paper"   -> A4 paper
    "printer paper"            -> Standard copy paper
    "A4 glossy paper"          -> not carried (A4 paper is not glossy; Glossy paper has no A4 size)
    "A3 paper", "balloons"     -> not carried
- Bulk discount by total units in the order: under 1,000 -> 0%, 1,000-4,999 -> 5%,
  5,000-9,999 -> 10%, 10,000 and above -> 15%.
- The customer accepts the quote immediately (the test harness has no back-and-forth).
- Partial fulfilment: unavailable items are left out with a reason; the rest is still sold.
- Packs are converted before stock, pricing, and the sale: paper is priced per sheet and a ream
  holds 500 sheets, so "500 reams of printer paper" is 250,000 sheets of Standard copy paper.
  The reply shows both ("500 reams (250,000 sheets) at $0.04 per sheet"). Packs with no standard
  size (packets, boxes, cases, ...) are marked needs clarification and the customer is asked how
  many sheets or items each holds.
- Delivery dates: an item in stock is ready on the request date, a restocked item on the supplier
  date. The order's delivery date is the latest of these and must be on or before the needed-by
  date. A supplier date after the deadline is offered only as an alternative, never confirmed.
- Replies must not reveal cash balance, inventory value, or margins.


4. WHY THE RULES LIVE IN THE TOOLS
----------------------------------
The first full test run sold to only 1 of 20 requests. Agents passed the customer's wording to
get_stock_level (which matches names exactly and returns 0 for unknown names), so most items
looked out of stock, and the one sale was recorded under an item name that does not exist.
Later runs showed that whenever numbers or names were copied from one agent to another as text,
the models sometimes changed them: a price 100x too high, a restock quantity used as the order
quantity, a delivery date used as the sale date, or a substitute item offered against the rules.

So the deterministic parts are in code and the agents only choose the next step:
- Every tool resolves item names with the same matching rule and rejects unknown items.
- calculate_quote and fulfill_order share one pricing function, so the recorded price always
  equals the quote.
- REQUEST_CONTEXT carries the request and needed-by dates, the available items with quantities
  in catalog units and their ready dates, and the recorded sale. Each item is checked once (asking
  again returns its first row), each quantity in the customer's text can be checked only once (so
  no substitutes), and a request is sold only once. Inventory checks and sales run under a lock,
  because the models sometimes call these tools in parallel (one run recorded a sale twice).
- Within a request, calculate_quote and fulfill_order always cover every available item, whatever
  item names the agent passes (another run sent one fulfill_order call per item and only the first
  item was sold, without its bulk discount).
- Unit conversion happens once, in check_inventory, so stock, restock, quote, and sale all use
  the same converted quantity. check_inventory also rejects quantities or pack units that are
  not in the customer's text: in one run the inventory model turned "200 sheets" into "1 ream"
  on its own, which sold 500 sheets. The delivery date also comes from check_inventory: an earlier
  version let the Sales Agent re-estimate it from the sale quantity, which promised an in-stock
  item three days after the customer's deadline.
- Stock checks also count transactions dated after the check date, so stock never goes negative.
- The Orchestrator's final answer checks enforce partial fulfilment and require the reply to
  give the sale's delivery date, total, quantities, and unit prices as fulfill_order recorded
  them, and the customer's real needed-by date (one reply called the day before it the deadline).


5. EVALUATION OF test_results.csv
---------------------------------
All 20 requests in quote_requests_sample.csv (2025-04-01 to 2025-04-17) were processed with no
errors by the final version of the code.

- 15 requests were fulfilled, fully or partly; 5 were declined with a reason (#8, #9, #11, #13,
  #15).
- Every reply was checked against the database. The items sold are exactly the items the
  inventory check marked available, recorded under catalog names and catalog prices with the
  correct discount, and each reply gives the recorded total, delivery date, quantities, and unit
  prices. No sale was recorded twice and no item's stock went negative.
- Cash went from $45,059.70 to $43,890.25; inventory value went from $4,940.30 to $4,398.40.
  26 sale lines brought in $11,225.15 and 20 stock orders cost $12,394.60. Most of the drop is
  #3: restocking 250,000 sheets of Standard copy paper and 9,863 sheets of A4 paper cost
  $10,493.15, and the order sold for $8,925.00 after its 15% discount (see improvement 1). The
  cash balance changed after 10 of the 20 requests.

Examples:
- #3  10,000 sheets of A4 paper + 500 reams of printer paper (500 x 500 = 250,000 sheets of
      Standard copy paper) = 260,000 sheets, 15% discount ($8,925.00). The reply shows
      "500 reams (250,000 sheets)" at $0.04 per sheet. Both items were restocked and are ready
      2025-04-11, before the needed-by date of 2025-04-15. "A3 paper" is not carried.
- #18 Colored paper (200 sheets) was in stock, so it is ready on the request date, 2025-04-14,
      one day before the needed-by date, and the reply gives that date. White cardstock and
      standard printing paper could only arrive on 2025-04-18; they were not sold, and the reply
      offers that date as an alternative for the customer to accept.
- #5  "8.5"x11" colored paper" is not carried (Letter-sized paper is not colored); Cardstock
      and washi tape were sold ($85.00).
- #6  Construction paper, Standard copy paper, and Cardstock = 1,000 sheets, 5% discount
      ($73.15).
- #20 5,000 Flyers + 2,000 Poster paper = 7,000 units, 10% discount ($1,125.00); "tickets" are
      not carried.
- #14, #16, #17 Partial orders: the available item was sold (Cardstock, Poster paper, Paper
      plates) and the customer was told which items the supplier could not deliver in time.
- #13 Declined: A4 paper and Cardstock were short and the supplier could deliver only on
      2025-04-12, after the needed-by date of 2025-04-10.
- #8, #11 Declined: every item is a size or finish the catalog does not carry (A4 glossy,
      A4 matte, A5 colored, A3 glossy, ...).

Strengths
- Data integrity: names, quantities, units, prices, discounts, stock levels, restocks, and
  delivery dates follow the rules in every run, whatever the models write.
- Replies give the values that were recorded; the final answer checks make the Orchestrator
  rewrite a reply that gives another delivery date, total, quantity, unit price, or deadline.
- Partial orders are handled: customers get what is available and a reason for the rest.
- The replies are customer-facing and do not reveal cash or inventory value.

Limitations
- The models are not deterministic, so the wording of replies changes between runs. Some
  replies mention stock counts, which the customer does not need.
- The strict size rule turns away many requests for sizes the catalog lacks (A3, A5, A4 glossy).
- Quantities and units are checked against the customer's text, but item descriptions are not,
  so a model's paraphrase can change a match (one run sent "colored construction paper" for
  "colorful construction paper", which is not carried).
- The reply checks look for the recorded values in the text; they do not catch every extra
  statement, e.g. #14 calls restocked Cardstock "in stock".


6. SUGGESTED IMPROVEMENTS
-------------------------
1. Earn a margin on restocked items. Stock orders are paid at the catalog price, which is also
   the selling price, so a restocked item earns nothing and a discounted one loses money
   (#2 sold $140.00 of goods and cash did not change; #3 lowered cash by $1,568.15). Add a supplier
   cost below the catalog price, or let the Quoting Agent limit the discount on restocked items.
2. Offer alternatives instead of declining. For an item that is not carried, the Inventory
   Agent already knows the closest item (e.g. Glossy paper for "A4 glossy paper"). A
   customer-confirmation step could offer it, which would turn several declined requests into
   sales.
3. Offer partial quantities and backorders. When stock is short and the supplier is late, the
   customer could get what is in stock now and the rest on the supplier date (#16 had 135 of
   the 500 A4 sheets requested).
4. Build the final reply from structured data. The final answer checks already make the reply
   give the recorded values; a reply template filled from REQUEST_CONTEXT would also keep the
   format consistent and leave out internal details such as stock counts.
